## Current workflow

- **Opportunity:** When the training room setup changes, I collect trainers’ written tips about connecting a laptop and choosing the display input. I compare the tips with the room setup, write a one-page guide, and share the checked version with our trainers. It takes about 45 minutes when the steps are scattered across several notes. I’d like an AI tool to draft the guide from notes we give it, with a source note for each step. It should flag disagreements and leave out details it can’t verify. Our training lead would check the draft against the notes and try the steps in the room before sharing. This is for the classroom display only.
- **Current steps:** From the opening answer (start) — “When the training room setup changes” From the opening answer (current steps) — “I collect trainers’ written tips about connecting a laptop and choosing the display input” From the opening answer (finish) — “and share the checked version with our trainers”
- **Volume and effort:** I revise the guide when the equipment or setup changes, around six to eight times a year. Writing and checking the first version takes about 45 minutes. For a trial I’d time the whole job, including reading the sources and trying the steps, to see if the draft saves at least 15 minutes.
- **Tools and handoffs:** I would paste the sample notes into a drafting tool that IT approves for the test, then copy the checked text into our guide template by hand. There’s no connection to the display, to any file store, or to a system that handles calls.
- **Inputs:** The inputs are short notes written in people’s own words, sometimes as a list and sometimes as a paragraph. I can label them Note A, Note B, and so on, but the wording and order vary. We’d give the tool only the handful of notes for one room setup.
- **Expected result:** I want a one-page draft with a ‘Before you start’ list, numbered setup steps, and a ‘Needs a check’ box. Each step should point back to the note it came from. The draft should leave a disputed detail out of the steps and put both versions in the check box. Staff make any updates to the guide by hand.
- **Exceptions:** If notes conflict, show both and flag the point for a trainer to check in the room. If there’s a missing step or a note is too vague, mark it as missing rather than filling it in. For example, if one note says to pick HDMI 1 and another note says HDMI 2, flag both input names for a room check before including an input in the guide.
- **Ownership:** The training lead owns the guide and the setup notes. They’ve approved trying the idea with made-up notes and want IT to confirm what tool we can use. The lead still signs off on any version people will use.
- **People affected:** The training resources coordinator prepares the draft. The training lead reviews it and runs the steps in the room. The small group of trainers uses the checked one-page guide.
